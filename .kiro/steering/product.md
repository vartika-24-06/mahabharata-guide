# product.md

## What it is
A Mahabharata-based guide that answers questions and shares stories from the
epic, grounded in its text and philosophy.

## Audience
Readers who want a mature take on the epic. "Mature" means engaging directly
with themes like greed leading to war and wiping out a lineage, the
consequences of actions, and extreme trickery in pursuit of goals.

Tone: There is no moralising. There is no explicit portrayal of right versus
wrong or hero versus villain.

## Core user journeys
1. Entry: the user wants a short, engaging story with no other intent. They are
   told this is about the Mahabharata and pick: hear a story, or ask a question.
2. Story mode: the user types a request, picks from popular or lesser-known
   characters or parvas, or asks to be surprised. They get a short snippet, then
   choose: already known, tell me more, or a new character/parva. They can
   switch to Q&A at any point.
3. Q&A mode: the user asks a factual or philosophical question. Intent is
   classified as factual, philosophical or ambiguous. Factual and philosophical
   answers cite exact sources. Ambiguous queries get two opening sentences (one
   per lens), each cited, then "tell me more" fetches the full relevant content.
   The user can follow up or switch to story mode.

## Out of scope (v1)
- Any question not answerable from the Mahabharata source
- Modern-life dilemmas that do not use epic vocabulary. In v1, philosophical
  mode only answers queries that use explicit Mahabharata names, places or
  themes. Examples such as "should I buy more plants?" and "is it right to
  overeat?" are deferred to v2, along with the abstract-vs-concrete dilemma
  classifier they depend on.
- Persistent conversation memory across sessions
- Languages other than English
- Native mobile app (web only; mobile-first responsive is a design decision)
- First-person character personas; stories are narrated in third person
